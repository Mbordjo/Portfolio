ORIGINAL WORLD · DIALOGUE TREE / QUEST DESIGN

The Forgotten Tides

An interactive fantasy story built around player-led conversations, discoveries, and a four-guardian quest.

Twine: Harlowe Branching Narrative Quest Design

Project Description

This project began as a topic-based dialogue hub where players can choose what they want to ask an NPC and explore the conversation at their own pace. I expanded that foundation into a larger fantasy story called The Forgotten Tides, where the player speaks with a Royal Archivist, learns about the kingdom's history and four ancient guardians, and gradually uncovers a larger mystery.

The conversation is designed to reward curiosity. Players can investigate the mysterious girl, the Kingdom of Tides, the Fallen Kingdom, the guardians, and strange events happening across the territories. Learning particular pieces of information unlocks the Royal Request, which introduces the main quest: seek out the Leviathan, Ancient Dragon, Celestial Phoenix, and Crystal Warden. The player can choose the order in which to visit them, and a quest journal tracks discoveries and progress. Completing the guardian journey leads to an encounter with Arriguii (Guii), setting up the next chapter.

I built the experience in Twine using Harlowe, passage links, variables, and conditional passages to make dialogue and quest progression respond to the player's choices.

IMPLEMENTATION

Dialogue & Progression Systems

Conditional Topics

Variables record which subjects the player has learned about. The Royal Request is gated until the required history and guardian information has been explored.

Quest State

Separate completion variables track the Leviathan, Dragon, Phoenix, and Warden paths. Completed quests are reflected in the map and journal.

Flexible Quest Order

The player chooses the next guardian from the quest hub. Dialogue can change based on which guardian was encountered first or which other guardians have already been met.

Fallback Dialogue

Locked topics have responses that explain why information is not available yet, keeping the conversation usable even when a player tries an option early.

PROCESS NOTE · LOOKING BACK

Reflection

For this project, I wanted to create an interactive dialogue experience where players could explore topics at their own pace. I expanded the original topic hub by adding a quest line, turning it into a larger interactive fantasy story. I chose Twine with Harlowe because I had some familiarity with the tool and wanted to refresh my skills while working with branching dialogue and story variables.

One implementation decision I am particularly proud of was using conditional passages and variables to track the player's progress. This let dialogue options unlock or change depending on what the player had already explored. I also added fallback responses for locked topics, and built the guardian quest system so players could choose which guardian to visit first instead of being forced into one order.

One challenge was organizing all the passages and keeping track of the conditions needed to move through the story. With multiple dialogue branches, unlocked topics, and guardian quests, I had to carefully manage variables and passage connections. I also struggled with whitespace formatting in Twine, which may cause some visual inconsistencies in passages. I experimented with a click-anywhere-to-continue interaction, but it did not work as intended, so I removed it.

Overall, this project helped me better understand how interactive dialogue systems use variables, conditions, and branching paths to create more engaging player experiences.
